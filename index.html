Content Strategy Tools

A working reference for the frameworks content strategists actually use. Easily look up what each one is and when to reach for it. 42 tools across 8 categories.

Strategy & Foundation

  • The Content Strategy Quad — Halvorson’s scope model: content design and systems design around one core strategy.
  • Content Marketing Charter — Aligns goals, audience, pillars, and KPIs on a single master page.
  • Editorial Mission Statement — A one-sentence filter for every content decision you will ever make.
  • Content Pillars / Themes — The three to five durable themes that organize everything you publish.
  • They Ask, You Answer / The Big 5 — Answer the five questions buyers actually search before they will talk to you.

Discovery

  • Content Audit + Inventory — A full census and quality assessment of every asset you own.
  • ROT Analysis — The standard cull rule: flag what is Redundant, Outdated, or Trivial.
  • Competitive Content Gap Analysis — Finds the topics competitors cover that you do not.
  • Content SWOT — Strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats, scoped to content.
  • Content Marketing Maturity Model — A staged diagnostic: where your content operation is now, and what to fix next.

Audience

  • Audience Personas — Named composite profiles of the readers you are actually writing for.
  • Customer Journey Map — Maps what each persona needs at every stage of the experience.
  • Jobs-to-be-Done / Value Prop Canvas — Frames content around the task the reader is hiring it to complete.
  • Voice-of-Customer / Message Mining — Extracts exact customer language from reviews, calls, and support tickets.
  • See-Think-Do-Care (STDC) — Kaushik’s intent framework: plan content, offers, and KPIs by audience intent.

The Core

  • Core Strategy Statement + Messaging Framework — The single document that tells every writer what to say and in what order.
  • Brand Voice + Tone Guide — Articulates the brand personality, and how it flexes by situation.
  • Point of View (POV) — A structured, defensible recommendation you put in front of a client or stakeholder.
  • Brand Archetypes — Twelve universal characters that give a brand a personality people recognize.

Structure & Mapping

  • Content Matrix / Content Map — The grid: audience segment, funnel stage, format, and topic.
  • Topic Clusters / Pillar Map — A pillar page surrounded by interlinked cluster content for topical authority.
  • Keyword Map — Assigns target keywords to specific pages so no two pages compete.
  • Content Model / Taxonomy — A structured blueprint for content types, fields, and relationships.

Planning & Operations

  • Editorial Calendar — The dated, assigned plan that turns strategy into a publishing schedule.
  • Content Brief — The per-piece spec that gives writers what they need before they start.
  • Content Workflow / Process Map — The defined stages a piece passes through from idea to published.
  • RACI Matrix — Assigns Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed to every task.
  • Editorial Style Guide — The rulebook for grammar, punctuation, terminology, and formatting.
  • Contributor / Freelancer Guidelines — The document external writers and SMEs need to produce work that fits.
  • Idea Backlog + Prioritization (RICE/ICE) — A scored list that ensures the highest-value content gets made first.
  • Channel Strategy / Distribution Matrix — Defines which channels you publish to, and the strategic role of each.
  • The PESO Model — Integrates Paid, Earned, Shared, and Owned media into one distribution system.
  • Hero-Hub-Help — Google’s programming model: tentpole moments, regular series, always-on answers.
  • Repurposing / Atomization Matrix — Turns one pillar asset into its full ecosystem of smaller spinoffs.
  • Content Governance Plan — The long-term standards and ownership that keep content from decaying.

Measurement

  • KPI / Measurement Framework — Connects what you track to the business outcomes that justify the work.
  • Content Lifecycle + Refresh Plan — Sets audit cadence, update triggers, and a sunset policy for aging content.
  • Content Quality Scorecard — A pre-publish rubric that removes vibes from the quality decision.

Information Architecture

  • Information Architecture / Sitemap — How content is structured, labeled, and navigated across a site.
  • Card Sorting — A research method for learning how users actually treat your content.
  • The Core Model — Finds the pages where user tasks and business goals overlap, then designs paths in and out.
  • Content Design (the GOV.UK method) — Start from evidenced user needs, then choose the format that meets them.
